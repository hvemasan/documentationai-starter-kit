## One Engine, Five Questions

Every survey type is built on the same single-card engine. They differ in **what they ask** and **how the answer is scored** — but they share the same targeting, triggering, follow-up, and analytics machinery. You pick the type when you create a survey; the type is locked once the survey exists, so responses always mean the same thing.

<a id="nps"></a>
## NPS — Net Promoter Score

NPS measures loyalty with a single 0–10 question: *"How likely are you to recommend us to a friend or colleague?"*

Responses are grouped into three bands:

| Band | Score range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Detractors | 0–6 | Unhappy users at risk of churn |
| Passives | 7–8 | Satisfied but unenthusiastic |
| Promoters | 9–10 | Loyal advocates likely to refer |

Your headline **NPS score** is the percentage of promoters minus the percentage of detractors, on a scale from −100 to +100.

<Callout kind="tip">
  Ask NPS on a recurring cadence (for example, every 90 days) so you can watch the trend over time rather than reading a single snapshot.
</Callout>

<a id="csat"></a>
## CSAT — Customer Satisfaction

CSAT captures how a specific interaction felt, on a 1–5 scale: *"How satisfied are you with your experience?"* It defaults to a **star** rating style.

| Band | Score | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Dissatisfied | 1–2 | The interaction fell short |
| Neutral | 3 | Neither good nor bad |
| Satisfied | 4–5 | The interaction went well |

CSAT works best fired right after a discrete moment — a completed purchase, a resolved ticket, or a finished setup step — while the experience is fresh.

<a id="ces"></a>
## CES — Customer Effort Score

CES asks how much effort a task took: *"How easy was it to get what you needed?"* It uses an editable **1–7** scale by default, where higher means easier.

Unlike NPS and CSAT, CES is not banded. It reports an **average effort score**, so a rising average means your product is getting easier to use.

You can change the range in the editor — set any **lowest** value (0–9) and **highest** value (2–10), as long as the lowest is below the highest.

<Callout kind="info">
  Existing CES surveys keep whatever range they were created with. Only newly created CES surveys default to 1–7.
</Callout>

<a id="poll"></a>
## Poll

A poll asks one multiple-choice question and tallies the votes. Add two or more answer options, reorder them by dragging, and choose how respondents answer:

- **Single choice** — the respondent picks exactly one option.
- **Multiple choice** — the respondent can pick several options.

Results appear as a per-option breakdown with vote counts and percentages. Because a multiple-choice respondent can pick more than one option, the percentages in a multi-select poll can add up to more than 100% — each percentage is the share of respondents who picked that option.

<a id="open"></a>
## Open feedback

Open feedback collects free-text responses in the customer's own words. The question shows as the heading, and the answer box invites a written reply (for example, *"Write your feedback…"*).

There is no score to compute — instead, analytics presents every response as a readable, scrollable list of quotes with the respondent and date attached.

<Callout kind="tip">
  Pair a scored survey with an open follow-up. On NPS, CSAT, and CES you can enable a follow-up question so a low score also captures the *why* in the same card.
</Callout>
